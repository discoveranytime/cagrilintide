01The satiety copy

What Cagrilintide actually is

Cagrilintide is a lab-built, long-acting copy of amylin — a hormone your pancreas releases with every meal, right alongside insulin. Where GLP-1 drugs work mainly through the gut-brain axis, amylin speaks to a different branch of the brain’s appetite wiring.

Each bead is one amino acid. The highlighted stretch marks where chemists modified natural amylin to make it last.

A copy of amylin

Cagrilintide is a synthetic analog of human amylin, tweaked at a few positions so it survives in the bloodstream for about a week instead of a few minutes.

Not a GLP-1 drug

Different hormone, different receptor, different brain targets. Amylin quiets hunger mainly through the brainstem’s area postrema — a satiety hub that GLP-1 drugs only partly reach.

The combo: CagriSema

Its developer pairs cagrilintide with semaglutide 2.4 mg in one weekly treatment. The combination, called CagriSema, is what’s actually in phase 3 trials.

Plain English

Think of a meal as a group chat: insulin handles the blood sugar, and amylin sends the “we’re good, stop eating” text. Natural amylin deletes itself in minutes. Cagrilintide is that same text message with a week-long battery.

02Amylin’s backstory

The sticky history of amylin

Amylin was discovered because it gummed up the works — then spent decades as diabetes’ overlooked “other hormone” before obesity researchers realized what it could do.

Amylin went from lab curiosity to diabetes drug to obesity-trial headliner in under 40 years.
Found in the gunk

1987: researchers isolating the sticky amyloid deposits in diabetic pancreases found a new hormone inside them — amylin. A nuisance discovery that turned out to be a satiety signal.

First draft: pramlintide

2005: an amylin analog called pramlintide (Symlin) was approved for diabetes. It worked, but needed mealtime injections — inconvenient enough that it stayed a niche drug.

The weekly upgrade

2010s–20s: chemists built long-acting versions, and trials hinted amylin plus GLP-1 beats either alone. Cagrilintide is the weekly heir to pramlintide’s mealtime legacy.

Plain English

Amylin’s career arc: discovered as pancreatic litter, hired as a diabetes helper, then finally promoted to obesity headliner once someone thought to pair it with a GLP-1.

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

For an investigational drug, the file is thick — real phase 2 and phase 3 trials, thousands of participants. What’s missing is approval and long-term follow-up.

Phase 2: amylin alone works

Cagrilintide on its own produced dose-dependent weight loss clearly beating placebo — proof the amylin lever does something in humans, not just rats.

Phase 3: the combo raises the bar

In the REDEFINE-1 trial, CagriSema averaged roughly 23% weight loss at 68 weeks — among the largest numbers ever reported in an obesity trial, ahead of semaglutide alone.

Still investigational

No regulator has approved it. The phase 3 program is the evidence — and trials are not the same as a marketed medicine with years of real-world safety data.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

What the trials showed, what’s plausible from the biology, and what nobody can answer yet — in separate boxes, on purpose.

GI side effects

Observed in trials: nausea, vomiting, and GI upset were the most common adverse events — dose-dependent and mostly early, the familiar profile of appetite-drug trials.

Unregulated vials

Observed fact: gray-market “cagrilintide” is sold online with no quality oversight. Independent testing of research-peptide vials has repeatedly found wrong doses and impurities.

Thyroid tumor question

Theoretical: GLP-1 drugs carry a rodent thyroid-tumor signal and a boxed warning. Whether long-acting amylin analogs share any such risk is unanswered — it’s the kind of thing long-term surveillance is for.

Pancreas & gallbladder

Theoretical: slowed stomach emptying and weight-loss drugs as a class have raised questions about gallbladder and pancreas events. Amylin-specific data here is thin.

Years-long use

Unknown: trials run about a year. What a decade of nudging the brainstem’s satiety hub does — to metabolism, mood, or anything else — is entirely uncharted.

Special populations

Unknown: no data in pregnancy, limited data in adolescents or the elderly, and drug-interaction studies are incomplete. The label for these questions is blank until approval.

Plain English

Known: GI effects in trials and unregulated vials online. Theoretical: thyroid and gallbladder questions borrowed from neighboring drug classes. Unknown: everything about long-term use. A promising file with the last chapters unwritten.

The pattern across all four: every newer approach stacks more appetite pathways — and every stack trades bigger numbers for more side effects and more unknowns. Cagrilintide's contribution is a pathway the others barely touch.

Two separate appetite wirings, barely overlapping — combined into one weekly treatment in the REDEFINE trials.

Why researchers bother combining instead of just raising a dose: the two pathways barely overlap. Turning up one signal eventually hits diminishing returns (and more nausea); adding a second, separate "stop eating" signal is harder for appetite to route around. That's the whole thesis REDEFINE-1 tested.

Plain English

Think of appetite as a house with two doors. Semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide all push on variations of the GLP-1 door — cagrilintide is the only one knocking on the amylin door. CagriSema just knocks on both at once.

Understanding Cagrilintide Terminology

The scienceAmylin is a 37-amino-acid hormone co-secreted with insulin by pancreatic beta cells in response to meals. It promotes satiety through brainstem signaling, slows gastric emptying, and suppresses the post-meal spike of glucagon. In type 1 and advanced type 2 diabetes, amylin is deficient alongside insulin. Cagrilintide is a long-acting synthetic analogue engineered to mimic and extend amylin’s actions.

Plain EnglishAmylin is a 37-block hormone your pancreas releases together with insulin every time you eat. It tells your brain you’re full, slows down your stomach, and keeps the blood-sugar-raising hormone glucagon in check after meals. In diabetes, amylin runs low along with insulin. Cagrilintide is a long-lasting lab-made copy designed to do amylin’s job for a full week.

The scienceCagrilintide is a long-acting synthetic amylin analogue, engineered with fatty-acid modification to resist enzymatic breakdown and extend its activity to about a week from a single administration. It reproduces amylin’s three main actions — appetite suppression, slowed gastric emptying, and glucagon suppression — in a once-weekly format. It is investigational as a standalone drug and is the amylin half of the CagriSema combination. Its trial program includes the REDEFINE studies.

Plain EnglishCagrilintide is a lab-made copy of amylin with a chemical tweak — a fatty-acid attachment — that stops enzymes from destroying it quickly, stretching one dose to about a week. It copies amylin’s three jobs: quieting appetite, slowing the stomach, and holding glucagon down. It’s still investigational on its own and forms the amylin half of the CagriSema combo. Its big trials run under the REDEFINE program.

The scienceCagriSema is the investigational fixed combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide 2.4 mg, developed as a single weekly treatment for obesity. It pairs amylin-pathway appetite signaling with GLP-1-pathway signaling in one product. The combination is being tested in the phase 3 REDEFINE program, including REDEFINE-1 in obesity and REDEFINE-2 in type 2 diabetes. Plain EnglishCagriSema is the experimental combo of cagrilintide plus semaglutide (2.4 mg) — the amylin signal and the GLP-1 signal in a single weekly shot. Two appetite pathways, one product. It’s being tested in the big phase 3 REDEFINE trials: one for obesity, one for type 2 diabetes. It isn’t approved yet, and the headline ~23% weight loss is measured against a placebo group.

The scienceGLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a gut hormone released after eating that boosts insulin secretion, slows gastric emptying, and reduces appetite through brain signaling. It is the pathway mimicked by semaglutide, tirzepatide, and other approved obesity and diabetes drugs. In the CagriSema combination, the GLP-1 component (semaglutide) is the senior, already-approved partner. Amylin and GLP-1 are distinct but complementary satiety signals — which is the rationale for combining them.

Plain EnglishGLP-1 is a gut hormone released after you eat: it boosts insulin, slows digestion, and tells the brain to ease off hunger. It’s the pathway behind semaglutide, tirzepatide, and the other famous weight-loss drugs. In CagriSema, the GLP-1 half (semaglutide) is the experienced, already-approved partner. Amylin and GLP-1 are two different fullness signals — pairing them is the whole idea behind the combo.

The scienceThe area postrema is a small brainstem structure that is dense with amylin receptors and sits outside the blood–brain barrier, allowing it to sample circulating hormones directly. It integrates satiety and nausea signals, making it a key node in amylin’s appetite-suppressing effect. Because it monitors the blood for toxins as well as hormones, it is also the brain’s vomiting trigger zone — which helps explain why amylin-pathway drugs commonly cause nausea. It is where cagrilintide’s signal lands hardest.

Plain EnglishThe area postrema is a tiny brainstem structure packed with amylin receptors — and unusually, it sits outside the brain’s security barrier, so it can taste hormones straight from the blood. It processes fullness signals, which makes it the key relay for amylin’s appetite effect. But it also doubles as the brain’s vomit trigger, watching the blood for poisons — which helps explain the nausea these drugs cause. It’s where cagrilintide’s signal hits hardest.

The scienceSatiety is the feeling of fullness that terminates eating and governs how long a person stays satisfied after a meal. It is distinct from satiation, the fullness felt during a meal; satiety determines the interval before hunger returns. Both are regulated by gut hormones — including amylin and GLP-1 — acting on brainstem and hypothalamic circuits. Amplifying satiety signaling is the central design goal of cagrilintide.

Plain EnglishSatiety is the “I’m good” feeling that stops you eating and keeps you satisfied afterward. It’s different from the fullness you feel mid-meal — satiety is what decides how long before hunger comes back. Gut hormones like amylin and GLP-1 control it by signaling the brain. Turning up that fullness signal is exactly what cagrilintide is designed to do.

The scienceGastric emptying is the rate at which the stomach releases its contents into the small intestine. Slowing this rate prolongs the feeling of fullness after eating, which is one of amylin’s main appetite mechanisms. The same slowing can cause nausea, vomiting, and reflux when it goes too far — the principal gastrointestinal side effects of amylin- and GLP-1-pathway drugs. It is both a therapeutic lever and a tolerability limit.

Plain EnglishGastric emptying is simply how fast your stomach hands food off to the intestines. Slow it down and you feel full longer — that’s one of amylin’s main appetite tricks. But slow it too much and you get nausea, vomiting, and reflux — the classic stomach side effects of these drugs. It’s both the lever that makes them work and the limit on how hard you can pull it.

The scienceGlucagon is a pancreatic hormone that raises blood sugar by instructing the liver to release stored glucose — functionally insulin’s opposite number. After meals, amylin suppresses glucagon’s spike, preventing the liver from adding sugar to blood that is already being fed from the gut. Cagrilintide reproduces this suppression as one of its three core actions. Dysregulated glucagon contributes to the high blood sugar of diabetes.

Plain EnglishGlucagon is insulin’s opposite number: a pancreatic hormone that raises blood sugar by telling the liver to dump its stored glucose. After a meal, amylin tells glucagon to sit down, so the liver doesn’t pile sugar onto blood that’s already getting fed. Cagrilintide copies that move as one of its three core jobs. When glucagon misbehaves, blood sugar climbs — part of the diabetes problem.

It reproduces amylin’s satiety, gastric-slowing, and glucagon-suppressing effects, but its short half-life required injections with every meal, which limited its adoption. It proved the amylin pathway works in humans but showed the field what a weekly version needed to fix. Cagrilintide is its long-acting successor.

Plain EnglishPramlintide was amylin 1.0 — the first amylin drug, approved in 2005 under the name Symlin for diabetes. It did amylin’s three jobs (fullness, slower stomach, less glucagon) but wore off so fast it needed a shot with every meal, which is why it never caught on widely. The sciencePhase 3 trials are large clinical studies — often enrolling thousands of participants — that test whether a drug works and whether it is safe enough for regulatory approval. They are randomized and usually placebo- or active-controlled, and they represent the last major step before regulators decide. Most drugs that reach phase 3 still fail, so the stage is a test, not a coronation. CagriSema is currently in phase 3 via the REDEFINE program.

Plain EnglishPhase 3 is the final boss fight before a drug can be approved: huge trials, often thousands of people, randomized against placebo or an existing drug, testing whether it works and is safe enough. It’s the last big step before regulators decide — and most drugs that get this far still fail. CagriSema is sitting this exam right now through the REDEFINE trials.

The scienceA placebo is an inactive treatment — a “nothing” pill or injection — used as the comparison arm in clinical trials. The gap between the drug group and the placebo group is the honest measure of a drug’s effect, separating pharmacology from expectation, natural disease course, and trial participation effects. In obesity trials, placebo groups typically lose a few percent of body weight on their own. CagriSema’s reported ~23% weight loss is measured against its placebo arm, not against zero.
